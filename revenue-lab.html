What $1 trillion a month actually means

$1 trillion every month is $12 trillion a year. Rough comparisons, using approximate public figures:

Honest takeaway: no website, product or company has ever come close to this, and none can be "built" in one file. The good news: the exact same formula that makes $1T impossible makes $10K/month very achievable.

A realistic path that actually works

Revenue isn't generated by code. It's generated by solving a painful problem for people who will pay. The website is just the shop window.

  1. Find one painful problem Week 1–2

    Talk to 20 people in one niche (shop owners, students, freelancers). Ask: "What task wastes your time or money every week?" Write down the exact words they use.

  2. Pre-sell before you build Week 3

    Make a one-page site describing the fix and a price. If nobody pays or joins a waitlist, change the idea — you just saved months.

  3. Build the smallest useful version Week 4–8

    Solve one problem well. Manual work behind the scenes is fine at first ("do things that don't scale").

  4. Reach $1K/month ~35 customers at $29

    Get customers by hand: direct messages, communities, helpful posts. Learn why people buy and why they leave.

  5. Reach $10K/month ~345 customers at $29

    Find one repeatable channel (search, content, partnerships). Cut churn — keeping customers is cheaper than finding new ones.

  6. Reach $100K/month raise price or add plans

    Hire help, add higher tiers for bigger customers, and measure everything: visitors, conversion, churn, lifetime value.

Key words, explained simply

Conversion rate

Out of 100 shoppers walking past a lemonade stand, if 2 buy, the conversion rate is 2%. Typical websites convert 1–3% of visitors.

Churn

A leaky bucket. If 5% of customers cancel every month, you must keep pouring in new ones just to stay level.

Customer lifetime

On average a customer stays 1 ÷ churn months. At 5% churn that's 20 months, so one $29 customer is worth about $580.
